## Findings

### Low — an early concurrent `VibeVoiceStt.close()` still escapes as `KeyError`

The round-1 concurrent-close defect is fixed for the interval its new test exercises: once `asr_infer` has started, `end_utterance()` retains a local process handle, reaps the child, and returns `""`. There is still an earlier close race introduced by the held-model-descriptor lifecycle. In `voicelib/stt.py`, `end_utterance()` creates its temporary WAV and then indexes `self._held[VIBEASR_VAE]` and `self._held[VIBEASR_LM]`; `close()` can concurrently replace `_held` with an empty dictionary. No lock or closed check covers that interval.

Concrete reproduction against `296ccea`: I instantiated the real `VibeVoiceStt` with the test's fake runtime, started and fed a turn, and paused its worker inside `paths.ensure_private_dir`, immediately before temporary-WAV creation and the `_held` lookups. The main thread called `close()`, then released the worker. It terminated rather than hanging, but the result was `{'error': "KeyError('vibeasr-vae-encoder-i8_s.gguf')"}` instead of the documented quiet empty result; `worker_alive=False`. The existing `test_close_from_another_thread_ends_the_turn_quietly` waits until the child writes its PID, so it cannot cover this pre-launch interval.

This is low severity: the production daemon owns and closes its engine in the same worker's `finally`, and the race requires another caller to share and close the recogniser. Make the held-descriptor snapshot and close transition atomic (or treat a concurrent pre-launch close as cancellation), and add a synchronized test before the `_held` lookup.
